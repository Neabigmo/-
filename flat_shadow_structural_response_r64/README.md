# R64 — structural quadratic-response lemma

R64 extracts the first nonlocal structure behind the R59–R63 cutoff chain.
The finite audit validates the formulas through degree 20.  The all-degree
statement below is conditional on the full same-factor hierarchy; it is not a
claim that the canonical non-Gaussian tower has already been constructed.

## 1. Canonical tangent

Use the canonical head

`alpha1=a`, `alpha2=-a`, `alpha_n=0 (n>=3)`, `t=a^2`,

with the audited convention `beta_n=h_n/h_(n-1)`.  In the orthonormal Jacobi
basis, `beta_n(t)=n+O(a^2)`, so the first Jacobi variation is the finite-head
perturbation

`J_a = J_0 + a(|e1><e1|-|e2><e2|) + O(a^2)`.

For the Gaussian-normalized formal MGF

`F_a(z)=L_a[exp(zX-z^2/2)] = 1+aU(z)+a^2V(z)+O(a^3)`,

Duhamel expansion gives

`U(z)=z^3 integral_0^1 s(1-s) exp(-s(1-s)z^2)
       (1-s(1-s)z^2/2) ds`.

Writing `U(z)=sum u_(2m+1) z^(2m+1)/(2m+1)!`, coefficient extraction yields

`u_(2m+1)=(-1)^(m-1) m (m+1)!/2`, for `m>=1`.

Equivalently, the first-order Hermite response is completely fixed:

`[a] L_a[H_(2m+1)] = (-1)^(m-1) m (m+1)!/2`.

The audit checks this identity for degrees `3,5,...,19` against the exact
canonical moments.

## 2. Same-factor quadratic response

Let

`r_j(theta)=sqrt(2/3) cos(theta+2*pi*(j-1)/3)`, so `sum_j r_j^2=1`.

If the full same-factor identity holds formally,

`average_theta product_{j=1}^3 F_a(r_j z) = 1`,

then the coefficient of `a^2 z^(2n)` gives a universal convolution law.  Define

`A_(2n)=average_theta sum_j r_j^(2n)
       = 3*binomial(2n,n)/6^n`,

and

`C_(r,s)=average_theta sum_{i<j} r_i^r r_j^s`.

Then, for `n>=2`,

`[a^2]L_a[H_(2n)]/(2n)!
 = -(1/A_(2n)) sum_{r+s=2n, r,s odd, r,s>=3}
      u_r u_s C_(r,s)/(r!s!)`.

The coefficients `C_(r,s)` are finite exact root-of-unity sums, not numerical
angular scans.  The audit checks the identity for every even degree from 4
through 20, using the exact R63 degree-20 moment.

## 3. Norm-curvature response

Write `L_a=L_0+aL_1+a^2L_2+O(a^3)` and let `H_n` be the monic probabilists'
Hermite polynomial.  If `pi_n(a)=H_n+a q_n+O(a^2)` is the monic orthogonal
polynomial, first-order orthogonality gives

`q_n=-sum_{k<n} L_1[H_n H_k] H_k/k!`.

Therefore

`h_n(t)=n!+K_n t+O(t^2)`,

where

`K_n=L_2[H_n^2]-sum_{k<n} L_1[H_n H_k]^2/k!`.

Consequently the Jacobi norm ratio satisfies the exact local formula

`beta_n(t)=n+Lambda_n t+O(t^2)`,

`Lambda_n=beta_n'(0)=(K_n-nK_(n-1))/(n-1)!`.

The audit verifies this through `n=10`, including
`Lambda_10=-1481/21` from R63.  Thus the R59–R63 slopes are not isolated
high-degree accidents: they are values of one finite quadratic form generated by
the canonical tangent and the same-factor quadratic convolution.

## 4. What this proves and what it does not

The new structural result is a conditional all-degree response lemma: the
canonical finite skew head fixes the complete first-order odd Hermite tangent;
the full same-factor identity fixes the complete second-order even response;
and the norm-ratio slopes are then determined by one explicit quadratic form.

This is stronger than a list of `P_6,P_8,Q_9,A_10`: it identifies the common
mechanism and gives a reusable test for any later cutoff certificate.  It does
not by itself control the `O(t^2)` remainder, prove that all even cutoffs shrink,
or bridge a Favard auxiliary law to an actual backward-OU preimage.

The next structural target is to estimate the quadratic form and its remainder
uniformly in `n`.  A negative subsequence alone is insufficient; one needs a
uniform radius on which the sign persists.  If that fails, the route should
move from Gaussian-local curvature to a genuinely finite-`t` tail deficit.
